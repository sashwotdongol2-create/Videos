# The Zack D. Films Shorts Formula: Script, Camera, Editing, Sound

Measured from 34 Shorts (@zackdfilms), with every frame analysed at native 29.97 fps (≈36,000 frames). The numbers come from the **26 strong videos**: the 20 all-time most viewed (105M–255M views) plus the 6 fastest-growing recent uploads (0.9–2M views/day, Sept–Oct 2026). Ranges are the middle half of those videos (25th–75th percentile).

The visuals (style, colour, characters, backdrop) are left out on purpose; bring your own. Everything below works with any look.

---

## 1. Script

### Hook (first sentence)

| Rule | Number |
|---|---|
| Narration starts on **frame 1** | first word at 0.00 s in 26/26 |
| The first sentence states the whole premise | **16.5 words** (14–20), spoken over the first ~6 s |
| Faster at the start | **3.0 words/s** in the first 3 s (3.0–3.7) vs 2.8 overall |
| Something is already happening when the voice starts | no strong video opens on a title, an intro or a still moment |

**Opening-line templates** (first sentence across the 26 strong videos):

| Template | Count | Real examples |
|---|---|---|
| **"If you [situation], …"** (or "If everyone / If [place]…") | 10 | "If you get a splinter, most people just go ahead and pull them out." · "If everyone on Earth clipped their toenails and threw them into the ocean, you might think they'd float…" |
| **In medias res**: a concrete action, no setup | 8 (**5 of the 6 newest winners**) | "A red hot iron is pushed into the tree, wounding it on purpose." · "He pulled the spring from his flashlight and bent it into a hook." · "A maggot dropped from her ceiling as she was changing her light bulb…" |
| **"When a/you [X], …"** | 4 | "When a firing squad shoots a prisoner, one of the bullets actually isn't real." |
| **"Have you ever noticed/wondered…"** | 3 | "Have you ever noticed how odd the McFlurry spoon is?" |
| **"Imagine…"** | 1 | "Imagine waking up in a coffin and realizing that you were mistakenly buried alive." |

**The misconception set-up** appears in 8 of 26 openings: "you might think…", "most people…", "you'd probably think…", "obviously…". It names the intuitive answer so the video can overturn it.

### Body

| Rule | Number |
|---|---|
| Length | **31 s** (29.5–35.4); newest winners run 35 s |
| Words | **85** (77–106) |
| Speech rate | **2.83 words/s ≈ 170 wpm** (2.61–2.97) |
| Narration coverage | voice over **98%** of the runtime; it never stops |
| Pauses | ~4 pauses over 0.3 s; **longest 0.45 s** (max 0.56 s) |
| Sentences | **5 sentences of ~17 words** (15–19): long and flowing, chained with "and", "so", "but", "which" |
| Reading level | grade 7.6 (6.5–8.5): simple words, long sentences |
| Questions in the narration | none (median 0) |
| Ending | a plain statement of the result; **0 calls to action**, no "subscribe", no "part 2" |

### Beat structure (start as % of runtime)

| Beat | Starts at | What the narration does |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0% | premise, with the misconception if there is one |
| Setup / turn | **~14%** (12–21%) | "But…", "Actually…", "you might think…"; the situation gets specific |
| Mechanism / escalation | **~47%** (33–61%) | "This is because…"; each step raises the stakes |
| Payoff | **~80%** (77–82%) | the answer, twist or survival in one line |
| Ending | **~94%** (92–95%) | one closing sentence; only 4 of 26 loop back to the opening line |

### Three story templates (pick one)

1. **Misconception → twist → mechanism** (splinters, armor, toenails, saw, water slide): "You'd think X. Actually Y. Here's why."
2. **"What if" escalation ladder** (blood vessels, sword, Alcatraz, grave bells): each step bigger than the last, ending on a scale comparison (a football field, Central Park, the Earth) or an open question.
3. **True story told from the middle** (toucan, 133 days at sea, maggots, 27-year walk): open on the strangest moment and reveal the backstory late ("you see, his ship had been torpedoed" arrives at 27 s). **This is what the newest winners use.**

More script rules:
- Hold back the famous name or the answer until the end ("Peter Parker" is only said in the last line).
- Use "you" in the hook, then switch to a third-person explanation.

**Don't:**
- Don't write a step-by-step how-to ("Keep your hands close together…") with no stakes or twist.
- Don't narrate a flat third-person case report that ends on a safety warning.
- Don't add an intro, a sign-off or a call to action.

## 2. Camera (measured by phase correlation on every frame pair)

| Rule | Number |
|---|---|
| The camera is **moving** | **93%** of the time (90–95%); static holds are only ~7% |
| Push-in (dolly / zoom in) | **63%** of runtime (56–71%) |
| Pull-out | **15%** (9–22%), used to reveal or resolve |
| Pan / tilt / sideways | **11%** (6–15%) |
| Push speed | **≈ 9% closer every second** (6–13%; 0.086 log-zoom/s) |
| Sideways drift | 0.044 frame-widths/s; only ~35% of all motion is sideways, so it's mostly straight in and out on one axis |
| Easing | every move eases in and out, never linear |

**The camera logic: start close, keep pushing in to explain, pull back out to resolve.**

Example, "How Stitches Work" (255M views): one continuous 31 s shot, zero cuts.
- 0–3 s: dolly in
- 4.5–9 s: hold while the action happens
- 9–12 s: slow creep
- 12–18 s: push in close
- 24–30 s: eased pull-out (speed −0.75 → −0.34 → −0.18 → −0.10 → −0.04)
- sideways movement ≈ 0 throughout

Camera rules:
- Lock moves to one axis (in/out), with ease-in and ease-out curves.
- Hold only while something is moving in the frame; otherwise keep creeping in.
- Save the big pull-out for the payoff (~80%).

## 3. Editing

| Rule | Number |
|---|---|
| Scenes per video | **5** (2–9) |
| Scene length | median **4.8 s** (3.2–13.8 s); longest ~13 s |
| Transitions per minute | 8.6 (2.4–13.2); newest winners run faster, at ~13/min |
| First transition | at **7.6 s** (4.9–20 s), so the hook plays out in one shot |
| **Hard cuts** | only **12%** of transitions (14 of 119), about **1 in 8** |
| Frame rate | true **30 fps** render (no duplicated frames) |

**How scenes change** (119 real transitions, each labelled by Claude from 9-frame strips):

| Transition | Share |
|---|---|
| Morph / dissolve (one image transforms into the next) | **29%** |
| Whip pan (directional motion blur) | **23%** |
| Zoom-through / fly to the next scene (the camera pushes through a surface into a new place) | **19%** |
| Card flip / rotate (the frame tilts or flips to reveal the next scene) | **13%** |
| Hard cut | **12%** |
| Wipe (something passes in front of the lens) | **5%** |

A further 177 moments looked like cuts to the detector but were really the camera travelling continuously. **The rule: the camera travels instead of cutting.** Hard cuts are kept for moments that are themselves sudden: a yell, a dice result, a switch between inside and outside.

**Captions:** 1–3 words at a time, on screen ~97% of the runtime, changing with the words as they're spoken. Style is your choice.

## 4. Sound

| Rule | Number |
|---|---|
| Integrated loudness | **−11.2 LUFS** (−12.0 to −9.1), louder than YouTube's −14 reference |
| Loudness range | **1.9 LU** (1.5–2.3): almost no dynamics, one flat wall of sound |
| True peak | about +0.65 dBTP (mastered hot; limit at −1 dBTP if you want to be safe) |
| Voice | −11.8 LUFS; the voice *is* the mix |
| Music / ambience bed | **18 dB under the voice** (16–20 dB), playing **89%** of the time |
| SFX hits | **~38 per minute** (25–67) |
| Transition sounds | about 1 in 3 transitions gets a whoosh or hit, landing **~1 frame before** the transition |
| Narrator | one calm, clear voice; pitch varies about ±6 semitones (lively, not monotone) |

## 5. Blueprint for a new video (31 s @ 30 fps = 930 frames)

| Time | Frames | Narration (≈2.8 w/s, ~85 words) | Camera | Editing | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–6 s | 0–180 | **Hook, ~16 words, starting on frame 1**: "If you…" or a mid-action line, plus "you might think…" if there is a misconception | Pushing in from frame 0 (~9%/s) | One continuous shot | Voice at full level; bed 18 dB under; one hit on the first action |
| ~4–8 s (14%) | ~150–240 | Turn: "But…" / "Actually…" | Keep pushing | **First transition** (whip, zoom-through or morph) | Whoosh ~1 frame before it |
| 6–15 s | 180–450 | Setup and stakes, 1–2 sentences of ~17 words | Push in; hold only while something moves | Scene about every 5 s | Hits on key actions |
| ~15 s (47%) | ~440 | Mechanism: "This is because…" | **Push through** into the inside view | Zoom-through | |
| 15–25 s | 450–740 | Escalation: each sentence a bigger step | Mostly one axis, eased | Morph / flip / whip every ~5 s; a hard cut only for a sudden moment | ~38 hits/min |
| ~25 s (80%) | ~740 | **Payoff line** | **Eased pull-out** to reveal | Hold the reveal | Biggest hit |
| 29–31 s (94%) | 870–930 | One closing statement; no CTA | Settle into a hold | No outro | Bed tails out |
